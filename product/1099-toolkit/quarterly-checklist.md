> # The Freelancer's Quarterly Tax Checklist
> ## What to do in each quarter so the IRS never surprises you
>

## Every month (5 minutes)

- [ ] Open your **Deductible Expense Tracker** and add anything you spent on the business that month.
- [ ] Open your **Mileage Log** and log every business trip you took that month. (Easier if you do it weekly.)
- [ ] Pull your invoice/payment records and add your gross 1099 income for the month to the calculator.

## January (Q4 of prior year + planning the new year)

- [ ] **Pay Q4 estimated taxes by January 15.** This is for the prior tax year. Use the Quarterly Estimated Tax Calculator's Q4 number.
- [ ] Update your Quarterly Estimated Tax Calculator with the new year's IRS standard mileage rate (verify on IRS.gov).
- [ ] Update tax brackets in the calculator for the new tax year.
- [ ] Review prior-year income — was your tax liability higher than expected? Adjust this year's estimated payments upward.
- [ ] If you collected 1099-NEC payments from clients, you'll receive them by January 31. Cross-check against your records.
- [ ] If you paid contractors more than $600 each, you must issue them **1099-NEC** by January 31. Use a tool like Track1099 or have your CPA file them.

## April (Q1 + the big filing)

- [ ] **Pay Q1 estimated taxes by April 15.** Same day as the annual filing.
- [ ] **File your prior year tax return by April 15.** Or file an extension to October 15 — but extensions only buy you time to *file*, not time to *pay*. Estimate your liability and pay it on April 15 either way.
- [ ] If you owed a big balance this year, increase your Q1 payment by 10–25% as a buffer.
- [ ] Open a separate "tax savings" bank account if you haven't already. Move 25–30% of every 1099 payment into it as it comes in.

## June (Q2)

- [ ] **Pay Q2 estimated taxes by June 15.** (Yes, only two months after Q1. Easy one to forget.)
- [ ] Review YTD income against your projection. If you're tracking 25%+ above projection, talk to a CPA about an S-corp election for next year.

## September (Q3)

- [ ] **Pay Q3 estimated taxes by September 15.**
- [ ] Check your retirement contribution YTD. If you have a solo 401(k), make sure you're on track to max it.
- [ ] If you're projecting to be over $80K in net SE income, schedule a tax-planning call with a CPA *before* year-end. The big moves (S-corp election, retirement plan establishment, big equipment purchases) have year-end deadlines.

## December (year-end planning)

- [ ] Buy any business equipment you need *before* December 31 to claim it this tax year (Section 179).
- [ ] Make your retirement contribution. Solo 401(k) employee deferrals must be made by December 31; employer contributions can extend to filing deadline.
- [ ] Pay all outstanding business expenses you can — cash basis means deductions count when paid, not when invoiced.
- [ ] Verify all contractor information for 1099-NEC: name, address, EIN/SSN. You need this in January.
- [ ] Schedule a tax-planning call with a CPA before December 31 if you haven't already.

## A few rules that save people from penalties

**Safe harbor:**
Pay the smaller of:
- 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your prior-year AGI was over $150K), or
- 90% of this year's actual tax

…in quarterly payments, and the IRS won't charge an underpayment penalty even if you owe more at filing. The Quarterly Calculator in this toolkit defaults to safe-harbor whenever it's the higher payment.

**Underpayment penalties stack:**
Skipping a quarter doesn't just delay the bill — the IRS charges interest from the date the payment was due. Pay quarterly even if you have to estimate roughly. A guess is better than a skip.

**State quarterly deadlines often differ:**
Maryland's deadlines align with federal. Many other states don't. Verify your state's deadlines at your state revenue website each January.
